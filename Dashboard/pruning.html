Splitting Criteria Explained

Gini Impurity measures the probability of misclassifying a randomly chosen element. It prefers binary splits and is computationally faster. Entropy (Information Gain) is based on Shannon entropy — it is slower to compute but can produce slightly more balanced trees. In practice both criteria often give similar results, with differences visible mainly at extreme depths.

Post-Pruning via min_samples_leaf

Post-pruning via min_samples_leaf prevents memorization by forcing each leaf to represent at least N training samples. Larger values produce smaller, more generalizable trees — but too large a value causes underfitting. The sweet spot balances leaf purity and generalization.

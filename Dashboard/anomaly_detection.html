IsolationForest

Builds random trees by repeatedly splitting on random features at random thresholds. Points that are isolated early (in shallow trees) are anomalies — they're easier to separate from the rest because they lie in sparse regions. Fast and scales to large datasets.

Local Outlier Factor (LOF)

Compares each point's local density to its neighbours' densities. A point surrounded by dense neighbours but lying in a sparse region scores high (anomalous). More sensitive to local anomalies — finds outliers that IsolationForest might miss in locally sparse patches.

Interpreting the Scores

IsolationForest: Score = negative average path length across trees. Higher score = isolated earlier = more anomalous. The threshold at contamination={{ data.contamination }}% splits the tail from the bulk.

LOF: Score = ratio of local density of point to density of its neighbours. Score near 1 = normal. Score >> 1 = much less dense than neighbours = anomaly.
